## Course Description

Join the Fall 2026 AI Innovation Lab cohort. In this project-based course, you will gain hands-on experience with emerging AI technologies, work with stakeholders on real-world challenges, and collaborate closely with other students. Together with your peers, technical advisors, and university partners, you will tackle institutional problems and contribute to Cornell's mission in administration, education, and research.

Each semester, Cornell units submit authentic problems in education, research, and administration. Your team will scope a problem, design an agentic-AI solution, and deliver a working product — culminating in a public Demo Day. Building on Agentic AI Architecture, you will apply large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, tools, memory, and multi-agent patterns under production constraints: data access, cost, evaluation, safety, and human oversight.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this semester, you will be able to:

1. **Scope** an authentic stakeholder problem and translate it into a feasible agentic-AI solution design with clear success criteria.
2. **Build, evaluate, and deploy** a working AI agent as part of a team, applying production constraints such as data access, cost, evaluation, safety, and human oversight.
3. **Communicate** results to non-technical stakeholders and critically reflect on the impact, ethics, and limitations of community-engaged AI work.
4. **Master core AI engineering techniques** — RAG, model selection, tool utilization, agents, and evaluations.
5. **Iterate rapidly**, develop functional prototypes, and successfully integrate stakeholder feedback.
6. **Thrive** in a fast-paced, highly collaborative agile team environment.
7. **Present** complex technical solutions to a large, diverse audience, including Cornell leadership.

## Student Expectations

### Attendance

Attendance at all scheduled sessions — weekly sprint meetings with stakeholders, weekly check-ins with Tech Leads, and the all-hands meeting — is mandatory. You get **2 free excused absences per recurring meeting type** throughout the semester. After that, points are deducted from your attendance grade.

### Engagement

Showing up is the baseline, not the bar. Come to meetings prepared. Offer comments, ask questions, give feedback, and contribute actively. Engagement is scored as part of each meeting type.

### Communication

- Be responsive on Teams — acknowledge messages within 24 hours
- Do not overburden stakeholders and Tech Leads — batch your requests and come with specific asks
- If you commit to a deadline, hit it or flag the delay early

### Team Conflict Resolution

Conflicts should first be resolved within the team. If unresolved, your TA will mediate. If still unresolved, the issue escalates to the instructor. Individual grades may be adjusted when a student disrupts team progress.

## Academic Integrity

All students are expected to uphold the [Cornell University Code of Academic Integrity](https://theuniversityfaculty.cornell.edu/academic-integrity/).

### What is and is not allowed

This is an AI engineering course, so we hold a deliberate position on AI tool use:

✅ **AI coding assistants** (Copilot, Cursor, ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) are **permitted and expected** for writing code. You are responsible for understanding, testing, and being able to explain any code you commit.

✅ **AI tools may be used to brainstorm and edit** (but not write) your individual deliverables. Your Weekly Reflections (A1), Peer Evaluations (A3), and your portion of the Final Presentation (F3) must represent your own thinking and writing. You may use AI to help you ideate, organize, and copy-edit.

❌ **Code reviews must be your own analysis.** Copying another reviewer's comments or using an LLM to generate review comments without genuine engagement counts as academic dishonesty.

❌ **Client data is confidential.** Do not paste stakeholder data, internal documents, or non-public credentials into third-party AI services. Use only the model providers approved by the course or your client. See [Responsible AI & Data →](resources/responsible-ai.md)

When in doubt, ask your TA **before** you do it.

## Accommodations & Accessibility

Cornell University is committed to providing equal educational opportunity and full participation for students with disabilities.

- **Registered with SDS:** Share your accommodation letter with the instructor and TA as early as possible. We will implement your approved accommodations across meetings, deliverables, and the final presentation.
- **Not yet registered:** Contact [Student Disability Services](https://sds.cornell.edu/) to discuss eligibility. Accommodations are not retroactive.

- **Documented illness or family emergency:** Contact your TA as soon as you can.
- **Varsity athletics, military service, or university-sanctioned travel:** Share your schedule in Week 1.

Concerns about harassment or discrimination can be raised with your TA, the instructor, or [Cornell's Office of Institutional Equity and Title IX](https://titleix.cornell.edu).
